# Core Lesson 5 — Perception vs. Fact

## Corrections Track

### 1. Core Connection

**What you perceive and what you know are not always the same thing.**

Perception is the meaning you give to what you see, hear, or experience.

Fact is what can actually be established.

A perception can feel completely real and still be incomplete or wrong.

The lesson is not:

> “Ignore your instincts.”

It is:

> **Learn to check your interpretation against what you actually know.**

### 2. Environmental Translation

In corrections, people may have strong expectations about authority, staff motives, other participants, and what will happen next.

Those expectations can shape how neutral events are interpreted.

A participant may see an ordinary interaction as a setup because previous experiences taught them to expect one.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A staff member asks a participant to come to an office.

The participant immediately thinks:

> “I'm in trouble.”

They become angry before learning why they were called.

The fact is only that they were asked to come to the office.

The rest is interpretation.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when your mind fills in information you do not actually have.

**Name**

> “I know what happened. I do not yet know what it means.”

**Explain**

Past experiences can make certain interpretations feel obvious.

That does not make the interpretation a fact.

**Separate**

Separate:

* what I observed
* what I assumed
* what I know
* what I still need to find out

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What do I actually know?”**

Then:

> **“What am I adding?”**

**Practice**

Before reacting, ask for information:

> “What is this about?”

Listen to the answer before deciding what the situation means.

**Carry It**

> **“A strong perception is still something I can check.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Checking my assumptions before acting on them.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot know another person's motives simply because I have a strong feeling about them.

### 7. Next Action

When I notice myself predicting what someone is going to do, identify one fact I actually have.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Check the facts before acting on the story.**

## Reentry Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Perception is not automatically fact.**

The meaning we assign to an event can influence what we do next.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Reentry can involve uncertainty.

A person may receive a rejection, unanswered phone call, delayed response, or unfamiliar instruction and immediately interpret it as proof that someone is against them.

That interpretation may be understandable.

It still needs to be checked.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

Someone applies for a job and does not hear back.

They immediately decide:

> “They saw my record and don't want me.”

They become angry and stop applying elsewhere.

They may eventually learn the position was filled before the application was reviewed.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when uncertainty becomes a definite conclusion.

**Name**

> “I do not know why they did not call me back. I know they did not call.”

**Explain**

Previous rejection can make a new rejection feel predictable.

That history can influence perception.

It does not prove the current interpretation.

**Separate**

Separate:

* what happened
* what I think happened
* what I know
* what I am assuming

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What other explanations are possible?”**

**Practice**

Continue pursuing reasonable options while gathering information.

Do not make one assumption responsible for the entire decision.

**Carry It**

> **“I can acknowledge the possibility of rejection without inventing certainty.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

How I respond to uncertainty and whether I allow an assumption to determine my next decision.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot know another person's motives without evidence.

### 7. Next Action

When something does not go as expected, identify the fact first and the interpretation second.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Uncertainty is not proof.**

## Veteran Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Perception and fact can overlap without being identical.**

Experience can sharpen awareness.

It can also influence what a person expects to see.

### 2. Environmental Translation

A person accustomed to reading situations quickly may make rapid judgments based on limited information.

That skill can be useful.

But civilian environments often contain ambiguity that requires gathering more information before deciding what something means.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A coworker suddenly leaves a meeting after receiving a message.

The participant assumes:

> “Something is wrong.”

They become concerned and begin changing plans based on that assumption.

Later they learn the coworker had a family matter.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when an interpretation becomes a conclusion before enough information exists.

**Name**

> “I noticed something. I do not yet know what it means.”

**Explain**

Rapid assessment can be useful when immediate decisions are required.

Not every civilian situation requires an immediate conclusion.

**Separate**

Separate:

* observation from interpretation
* possibility from probability
* awareness from certainty
* useful instinct from established fact

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What information am I missing?”**

**Practice**

Gather the missing information before changing plans or confronting someone.

**Carry It**

> **“Notice quickly. Conclude carefully.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Whether I turn an observation into an accusation or decision without sufficient evidence.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot know what another person is thinking or why they acted without information.

### 7. Next Action

Before acting on an assumption, identify what information would confirm or disprove it.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Observation is not conclusion.**

## Workplace Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Perception is an interpretation. Fact is what can be established.**

Workplace conflict often grows when people react to what they think someone meant instead of what was actually said or done.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Employees constantly interpret:

* emails
* facial expressions
* feedback
* meeting behavior
* silence
* tone
* decisions
* changes in responsibilities

A person can turn incomplete information into a complete story very quickly.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A supervisor sends a short email:

> “See me when you get a chance.”

The employee immediately assumes they are in trouble.

They spend the morning anxious and defensive.

The supervisor simply wanted to discuss a schedule change.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when a neutral fact becomes a negative story.

**Name**

> “The fact is that my supervisor wants to talk to me. The rest is my assumption.”

**Explain**

Previous experiences with criticism or punishment can make ambiguous communication feel threatening.

That explains the reaction.

It does not establish the conclusion.

**Separate**

Separate:

* the words that were actually said
* the meaning I assigned to them
* what I know
* what I fear

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What evidence supports my interpretation?”**

Then ask:

> **“What evidence contradicts it?”**

**Practice**

Wait for the conversation.

If clarification is appropriate, ask directly instead of building a conclusion from incomplete information.

**Carry It**

> **“Do not let an assumption become a fact just because it feels convincing.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Checking my interpretation before reacting and communicating directly when clarification is needed.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot know another person's thoughts or intentions without evidence.

### 7. Next Action

When I receive ambiguous communication, wait for information or ask a direct question before reacting.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Facts first. Story second.**
