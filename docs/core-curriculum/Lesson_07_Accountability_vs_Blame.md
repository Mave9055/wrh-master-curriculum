# Core Lesson 7 — Accountability vs. Blame

## Corrections Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Accountability and blame are different.**

Blame asks:

> “Whose fault is this?”

Accountability asks:

> **“What part belongs to me, and what do I do about it?”**

Accountability does not require pretending other people did nothing wrong.

It requires being honest about your own part.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Corrections environments can create strong conflict around responsibility.

Participants may believe:

> “They made me do it.”

Staff may believe:

> “He chose everything.”

Reality can be more complicated.

Circumstances influence behavior.

They do not erase personal choice.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A participant receives a consequence after an argument.

They explain:

> “The officer started it.”

That may be relevant.

But it does not answer:

> “What did you do once the conflict started?”

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when explaining someone else's behavior has replaced examining your own.

**Name**

> “Their behavior may have contributed. I still have to account for mine.”

**Explain**

People often focus on the other person's wrongdoing because it feels unfair to acknowledge their own part.

**Separate**

Separate:

* what they did
* what I did
* what I could not control
* what I could have done differently

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“Regardless of what they did, what part of this belongs to me?”**

**Practice**

State the facts without using them to escape responsibility:

> “I was provoked. I still chose to respond the way I did.”

Then identify what will change next time.

**Carry It**

> **“Someone else's responsibility does not cancel mine.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My choices, words, behavior, and response to pressure.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

Another person's choices and wrongdoing.

### 7. Next Action

Identify one behavior I can change regardless of what another person does.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Own your part without carrying theirs.**

## Reentry Track

### 1. Core Connection

Accountability is not the same as accepting all blame.

A person can acknowledge their mistakes without claiming responsibility for everything that happened around them.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Reentry may involve consequences connected to past decisions.

It can be tempting to explain everything through other people:

> “My family caused it.”

> “The system caused it.”

> “My circumstances caused it.”

Circumstances matter.

But rebuilding requires identifying the part that is actually yours.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A person misses an important appointment.

They blame transportation, the weather, the reminder system, and the person who gave them the appointment.

Some of those factors may be real.

The appointment was still missed.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when explaining circumstances has replaced solving the problem.

**Name**

> “There were obstacles. I also did not make a backup plan.”

**Explain**

Difficult circumstances can genuinely interfere with success.

Acknowledging them is not weakness.

Using them to avoid examining your own choices prevents change.

**Separate**

Separate:

* circumstances
* other people's responsibility
* my responsibility
* what I can change next time

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What would I do differently if I had to handle this again?”**

**Practice**

Create a backup plan.

Set an earlier reminder.

Arrange transportation sooner.

Ask for help before the deadline.

**Carry It**

> **“Accountability is not saying everything was my fault. It is identifying what was mine.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Preparation, planning, communication, and follow-through.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot rewrite circumstances or control another person's choices.

### 7. Next Action

Take one concrete step that prevents the same problem from happening the same way again.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Do not argue about all the blame. Find your part and work it.**

## Veteran Track

### 1. Core Connection

Accountability means owning what belongs to you.

It does not mean accepting blame for everything.

### 2. Environmental Translation

In team environments, people often depend on one another.

When something goes wrong, the instinct may be to identify who failed.

That can be necessary for understanding an event.

But personal accountability asks a different question:

> **“What was my part?”**

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A project fails because several people missed important steps.

The participant immediately focuses on the person who made the largest mistake.

Later, they recognize that they saw a problem earlier but did not speak up.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when identifying someone else's failure becomes a way to avoid examining your own.

**Name**

> “They made a mistake. I also saw part of the problem and stayed quiet.”

**Explain**

People naturally protect themselves when responsibility feels threatening.

**Separate**

Separate:

* their mistake
* my mistake
* what I knew
* what I did with what I knew

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What did I know, and what did I do about it?”**

**Practice**

Acknowledge your part without exaggerating it:

> “I should have raised the concern earlier.”

Then identify what you will do differently.

**Carry It**

> **“Owning my part does not require taking someone else's.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

What I knew, what I communicated, what I failed to communicate, and what I chose to do.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

Another person's decisions, mistakes, or responsibilities.

### 7. Next Action

When I notice a problem I have relevant information about, raise it through the appropriate channel.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Own your piece of the mission.**

## Workplace Track

### 1. Core Connection

Accountability focuses on responsibility and action.

Blame focuses on assigning fault.

A workplace needs accurate responsibility, not endless fault-finding.

### 2. Environmental Translation

When something goes wrong at work, employees may immediately defend themselves:

> “That wasn't my fault.”

That may be factually correct.

But the better question may be:

> **“What can I do now?”**

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A customer complaint results from a mistake made by another employee.

The participant did not cause the mistake.

Instead of helping resolve the problem, they spend the next twenty minutes explaining why it was not their fault.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when proving innocence becomes more important than solving the problem.

**Name**

> “I did not cause the mistake. I can still help fix the situation.”

**Explain**

People often defend themselves quickly because being associated with a mistake can feel threatening.

**Separate**

Separate:

* responsibility for causing the problem
* responsibility for responding to the problem
* another person's mistake
* my available contribution

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What part can I responsibly handle?”**

**Practice**

Correct what can be corrected.

Communicate what needs to be communicated.

Identify who owns the remaining responsibility.

Then move forward.

**Carry It**

> **“I do not have to accept blame to accept responsibility for my response.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My response, my communication, and whatever corrective action falls within my role.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I am not responsible for another employee's mistake simply because I am nearby.

### 7. Next Action

When something goes wrong, identify my actual responsibility before defending myself or blaming someone else.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Responsibility is about what you do next, not just who gets blamed.**
