# Core Lesson 6 — Control vs. Influence

## Corrections Track

### 1. Core Connection

There is a difference between **what I control** and **what I can influence**.

I do not control other people, institutional decisions, rules, schedules, or outcomes.

I do control my choices, behavior, preparation, communication, and response.

Knowing the difference prevents wasted effort and unnecessary conflict.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Corrections environments contain many things participants cannot change directly.

Rules may be fixed.

Schedules may be determined by others.

Staff decisions may not go the way a participant wants.

Other people's behavior may be frustrating.

Trying to control those things can turn frustration into conflict.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A participant becomes angry because staff will not change a decision.

They argue repeatedly, believing that if they push hard enough, the decision will change.

The decision does not change.

The participant receives additional consequences for the way they handled the disagreement.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when effort is being spent trying to control something outside your authority.

**Name**

> “I cannot control this decision. I can control what I do next.”

**Explain**

When a person feels powerless, pushing harder can create the feeling that they are taking control back.

The effort may be understandable.

It does not make the strategy effective.

**Separate**

Separate:

* what I control
* what I influence
* what I cannot change
* what I can choose in response

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What is actually mine to act on?”**

**Practice**

If a decision can be challenged through an established process, use that process.

If it cannot be changed immediately, stop fighting the fact of it and focus on the next available choice.

**Carry It**

> **“I do not need control of the situation to have control over my next move.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My conduct, communication, choices, and use of legitimate processes.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

Another person's decisions, institutional authority, or circumstances outside my control.

### 7. Next Action

Identify one thing I can influence and act on it instead of arguing about what I cannot control.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Control your response. Influence what you can. Stop fighting what you cannot.**

## Reentry Track

### 1. Core Connection

I cannot control every outcome.

I can influence outcomes through preparation, consistency, communication, and action.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Reentry can involve housing decisions, employment decisions, court requirements, family relationships, transportation, finances, and other people's choices.

Trying to control all of these at once can become overwhelming.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A person applies for several jobs.

They become angry because employers do not respond.

They spend hours thinking about what the employers should have done instead of submitting another application or improving their résumé.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when frustration is focused on an outcome that belongs to someone else.

**Name**

> “I cannot make them hire me. I can improve what I bring to the next application.”

**Explain**

Rejection can create a strong desire to regain control.

Focusing on another person's decision can feel easier than facing uncertainty.

**Separate**

Separate:

* the outcome I want
* the actions I control
* the factors I can influence
* the decisions other people make

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What can I do that improves my position?”**

**Practice**

Submit another application.

Improve one qualification.

Make one appropriate follow-up.

Ask for useful feedback.

Keep moving.

**Carry It**

> **“I cannot command an outcome. I can increase my chances through action.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Preparation, effort, follow-through, communication, and learning from results.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

Another person's decision, timing, priorities, or opinion.

### 7. Next Action

Choose one action today that increases the likelihood of the outcome I want.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Work the part you can actually move.**

## Veteran Track

### 1. Core Connection

Control and influence are different.

Strong people are not required to control everything.

They need to know where their responsibility and influence actually begin and end.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Military experience can involve defined roles, procedures, leadership structures, and missions.

Civilian life may contain far less control over surrounding conditions.

A person may become frustrated when people do not respond with the same discipline, urgency, or priorities they expect.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A civilian coworker repeatedly misses deadlines.

The participant becomes increasingly frustrated and tries to force the coworker to change.

The coworker remains responsible for their own work.

The participant is responsible for their own response.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when someone else's behavior is consuming more attention than your own responsibilities.

**Name**

> “I can influence this situation, but I cannot make this person behave the way I want.”

**Explain**

Structured environments can create clear expectations about roles and responsibilities.

Civilian environments may contain different levels of control and accountability.

**Separate**

Separate:

* my responsibility
* my influence
* another person's responsibility
* the organization's responsibility

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What is the appropriate level of influence here?”**

**Practice**

Communicate the problem.

Document what is necessary.

Use the proper chain or process.

Then return attention to what is actually yours.

**Carry It**

> **“Influence does not require control.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My work, communication, boundaries, and appropriate use of available processes.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot force another adult to make good choices.

### 7. Next Action

Address the issue through the appropriate channel, then stop carrying responsibility for another person's choices.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Do your part without taking ownership of someone else's.**

## Workplace Track

### 1. Core Connection

The workplace contains things I control, things I influence, and things I cannot control.

Confusing those categories creates frustration.

### 2. Environmental Translation

An employee may try to control coworkers, management decisions, schedules, customer reactions, or company policies.

The more useful approach is to focus effort on preparation, communication, performance, and appropriate escalation.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

An employee is frustrated by a coworker's behavior.

They repeatedly complain to everyone around them.

Their own work begins suffering.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when another person's behavior has become the center of your attention.

**Name**

> “I am spending energy trying to change something I do not directly control.”

**Explain**

Frustration can create the belief that if the other person would simply change, everything would be fine.

**Separate**

Separate:

* what I control
* what I can influence
* what management controls
* what another employee controls

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What is the most productive action available to me?”**

**Practice**

Communicate the issue appropriately.

Document relevant facts if necessary.

Continue performing my own responsibilities.

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My performance, conduct, communication, and response.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I do not control coworkers, customers, managers, or company decisions.

### 7. Next Action

Spend the next available block of effort on something I can directly improve.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Put your effort where your effort can matter.**
