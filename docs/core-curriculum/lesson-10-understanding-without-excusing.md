# UNDERSTANDING WITHOUT EXCUSING

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

done something you knew was wrong and later understood exactly why you did it?

Maybe you were angry.

Maybe you were scared.

Maybe you felt cornered.

Maybe you were exhausted.

You can explain what led up to it.

But there's still a fact sitting there:

> **You did it.**

Sometimes people use explanations in two different ways.

One person says:

> *"Now I understand what happened."*

Another says:

> *"So I couldn't help it."*

Those are not the same thing.

---

### 2. NAME

The difference is:

## **Explanation is not excuse.**

An explanation answers:

> **"What may have led to this?"**

An excuse says:

> **"Therefore, I don't have to take responsibility."**

WRH is interested in the first one.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Knowing what happened inside you can help you change what happens next.

Maybe you notice:

> pressure → anger → argument → worse consequences

Now you have a map.

Without the map, you may keep asking:

> *"Why does this shit keep happening?"*

But having a map doesn't erase the damage.

If you hurt somebody, they still got hurt.

If you broke a rule, the rule still exists.

If you missed something important, the consequence may still be there.

Understanding gives you information.

**It does not erase the bill.**

---

### 4. SEPARATE

You can hold two truths at the same time:

> **Something happened that affected you.**

and

> **You are responsible for what you choose to do with it.**

You don't have to choose between blaming yourself for everything and blaming everything on somebody else.

There is another position:

> **"I understand what happened. I own what I did. Now I need to decide what happens next."**

That's accountability.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

When you catch yourself explaining what happened, add one question:

> **"What is mine to own?"**

Not:

*"Whose fault is everything?"*

Not:

*"Who started it?"*

Just:

> **What part is mine?**

Then deal with that part.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Imagine this:

Someone says something disrespectful to you.

You snap back.

The conversation gets worse.

Later, you can see the sequence:

**They said something.**

**You reacted.**

**You made it worse.**

Practice separating the pieces.

Their words are theirs.

Your response is yours.

Now ask:

> **"What is mine to own?"**

Maybe the answer is:

> *"I should not have yelled."*

You don't have to decide whether their behavior was okay.

You are simply identifying your part.

That is where change starts.

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **Understanding why you did something can help you change it; it doesn't erase responsibility for doing it.**
