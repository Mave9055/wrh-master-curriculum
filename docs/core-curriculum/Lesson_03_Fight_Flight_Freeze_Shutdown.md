# Core Lesson 3 — Fight, Flight, Freeze & Shutdown

## Corrections Track

### 1. Core Connection

People can respond to pressure in different directions.

They may move toward conflict, move away, become still, or shut down communication.

These are patterns to recognize—not permanent boxes or excuses.

### 2. Environmental Translation

In corrections, pressure can produce different outward behaviors.

One person argues.

Another walks away.

Another becomes silent.

Another appears unable or unwilling to respond.

The behavior may look different, but the important question is:

> **What happened, what did I do, and what can I choose now?**

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A participant is confronted about a rule violation.

They stop talking completely.

Staff interprets the silence as deliberate refusal.

The participant knows they were overwhelmed and did not know what to say.

The result is still a failure to communicate.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice which direction your response is moving:

* toward conflict
* away from the situation
* into stillness
* into shutdown

**Name**

> “I shut down when the pressure increased.”

**Explain**

Pressure can narrow a person's ability to respond in the moment.

That may explain the silence.

It does not remove the need to communicate or address the situation.

**Separate**

Separate:

* inability to respond immediately from refusal
* explanation from excuse
* pressure from permission to violate rules
* another person's interpretation from your actual intention

**Interrupt**

Look for one small action in the opposite direction.

If you are shutting down:

> “I need a minute, but I will answer.”

If you are escalating:

> “Let me stop and think.”

**Practice**

Use one sentence that keeps the interaction moving without pretending everything is fine.

**Carry It**

> **“I may not control the first reaction. I can work on the next response.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My behavior, communication, and effort to return to the situation after the initial reaction.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot control another person's interpretation of my reaction.

### 7. Next Action

When pressure causes me to shut down, communicate one clear sentence instead of disappearing from the interaction.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **One small response is better than letting shutdown make the decision.**

## Reentry Track

### 1. Core Connection

People can respond to pressure by moving toward conflict, away from it, becoming still, or shutting down.

The first reaction is information.

It is not the final decision.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Reentry creates pressure from many directions:

* paperwork
* employment
* appointments
* housing
* family
* money
* rejection
* unfamiliar responsibilities

A person may fight every problem, avoid every problem, freeze when choices pile up, or shut down completely.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

Someone receives several important notices in the same week.

They become overwhelmed.

Instead of opening them, they put everything aside.

Days pass.

The problems become harder to solve.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice the move toward avoidance or shutdown.

**Name**

> “I am not solving this. I am avoiding looking at it because it feels too big.”

**Explain**

When several demands arrive at once, doing nothing can feel easier than choosing where to start.

**Separate**

Separate:

* overwhelmed from incapable
* delay from permanent failure
* one problem from every problem
* discomfort from danger

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What is the smallest thing I can handle first?”**

**Practice**

Open one letter.

Make one call.

Complete one form.

Write down one deadline.

Then stop or continue based on what is actually required.

**Carry It**

> **“I do not have to solve everything to start solving something.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Taking action on responsibilities instead of allowing avoidance to create additional consequences.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot control every demand, deadline, or decision made by another person or organization.

### 7. Next Action

Choose one unfinished responsibility and take one concrete step on it today.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **When everything feels too big, make the first thing smaller.**

## Veteran Track

### 1. Core Connection

Pressure can push a person toward conflict, escape, stillness, or shutdown.

Recognizing the direction creates a chance to choose a different response.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Someone accustomed to responding quickly under pressure may continue using the same pattern when civilian situations require communication, patience, or deliberation.

The opposite can also happen: a person who has spent years containing reactions may shut down instead of explaining what is happening.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A family member raises an emotional issue unexpectedly.

The participant becomes silent and leaves the room.

They are not trying to punish the person.

They do not know how to respond without making things worse.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice the move away from the conversation.

**Name**

> “I want out of this conversation because I do not know what to do with it.”

**Explain**

Leaving may have become a familiar way to regain control when pressure increases.

**Separate**

Separate:

* taking space from abandoning the conversation
* calm from silence
* privacy from avoidance
* control from connection

**Interrupt**

Say:

> “I need some time. I will come back to this.”

**Practice**

Take the space.

Then return when you said you would.

**Carry It**

> **“Taking space can be responsible. Disappearing does not solve the problem.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Communicating the need for space and following through on returning to the conversation.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I am not responsible for controlling another person's emotions or making every conversation comfortable.

### 7. Next Action

When I need space, state that clearly and give a reasonable time to return.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Take space without abandoning responsibility.**

## Workplace Track

### 1. Core Connection

Pressure can produce conflict, avoidance, stillness, or shutdown.

Recognizing the response creates room to choose a useful action.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Work pressure can show up as an argument with a coworker, leaving the task, freezing during a meeting, or becoming silent after criticism.

Different behaviors can produce the same result:

**the problem remains unresolved.**

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

An employee is asked an unexpected question during a meeting.

They freeze and say nothing.

Afterward, they avoid the supervisor because they are embarrassed.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when embarrassment turns into silence and avoidance.

**Name**

> “I froze because I did not know the answer.”

**Explain**

Not knowing an answer can feel threatening when mistakes have previously meant rejection or humiliation.

**Separate**

Separate:

* not knowing from being incapable
* embarrassment from danger
* a mistake from identity
* needing time from refusing responsibility

**Interrupt**

Use a simple response:

> “I don't have that answer yet. Let me find out.”

**Practice**

Give yourself permission to not know everything while remaining responsible for finding the answer.

**Carry It**

> **“Not knowing is a problem I can work on. It does not have to become a reason to disappear.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Being honest about what I know, finding the answer, and following through.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I am not responsible for knowing everything or controlling how others react to a mistake.

### 7. Next Action

The next time I do not know something, say so clearly and identify how I will get the answer.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **You can be uncertain without disappearing.**
