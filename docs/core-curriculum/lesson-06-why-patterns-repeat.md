# WHY PATTERNS REPEAT

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

promised yourself:

> *"I'm not doing that again."*

And then somehow you do it again.

Maybe you put off the same kind of phone call.

Avoid the same kind of conversation.

Get angry in the same kind of situation.

Stay up too late.

Spend money you meant to save.

Miss something important.

Then afterward you think:

> *"I knew better."*

You probably did.

That's what makes it so frustrating.

You can see the pattern afterward.

But somehow, when the moment comes again, you end up standing in the same place.

---

### 2. NAME

That's a:

## **Pattern.**

A pattern is something that happens in a similar way more than once.

It can be a behavior.

A thought.

A reaction.

A habit.

Or a combination of all four.

And patterns can become easier to repeat because your brain learns what usually comes next.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Think about a road.

The first time you walk it, you have to pay attention.

After you've walked it a hundred times, you don't have to think about every step.

Your brain gets better at following the route.

Behavior can work in a similar way.

Something happens.

You react.

The reaction gives you some kind of result.

Maybe the problem gets smaller for a few minutes.

Maybe you get relief.

Maybe the argument stops.

Maybe you don't have to deal with something yet.

Your brain notices:

> **"That worked."**

So the next time something similar happens, the same route becomes easier to take.

That doesn't mean the pattern is good.

It means **familiar actions can become automatic actions.**

---

### 4. SEPARATE

A pattern can explain repetition.

It does not make repetition unavoidable.

You are not required to keep doing something simply because you've done it before.

And you don't have to change your entire life in one day.

The first win is much smaller:

> **Notice the pattern while it is happening.**

Once you can see it, you have somewhere to start.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

Find the **first step** in the pattern.

Not the final disaster.

Not everything that went wrong.

The first step.

Maybe it's:

> *"I see the message."*

Then:

> *"I decide not to answer."*

That's the point to work with.

If you can change the first step, you may not have to fight the whole chain later.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Think of a completely ordinary situation:

**Message arrives.**

↓

**You don't answer.**

↓

**You think you'll answer later.**

↓

**You forget.**

↓

**Now answering feels awkward.**

↓

**You avoid it even longer.**

Don't tell anyone whether you've done this.

Just look at the pattern.

Where is the easiest place to interrupt it?

Maybe it's the first ten seconds.

You don't have to write the perfect response.

You could simply send:

> **"Got this. I'll get back to you."**

One small change.

One different route.

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **You don't have to break the whole pattern at once; change the first step you can see.**
