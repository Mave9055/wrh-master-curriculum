# Core Lesson 4 — Impact vs. Intention

## Corrections Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Intention and impact are not the same thing.**

You may intend one thing and still create a different result.

Your intention matters because it tells us what you meant to do.

The impact matters because it tells us what actually happened.

Understanding the difference does not mean choosing one over the other.

It means being honest about both.

### 2. Environmental Translation

In corrections, a participant may believe they are protecting themselves, showing respect, or preventing escalation while their behavior is experienced by staff as threatening, disrespectful, or defiant.

The participant does not have to agree with the interpretation to recognize the impact.

The question becomes:

> **“What did I mean, what actually happened, and what can I change?”**

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A staff member approaches a participant.

The participant steps backward, raises their voice, and says:

> “Back up.”

The participant intends to create space.

Staff experiences the behavior as confrontational.

The participant later says:

> “I wasn't trying to threaten anybody.”

That may be true.

It does not erase what the behavior communicated.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when your intention and the effect of your behavior are different.

**Name**

> “I was trying to protect my space, but my behavior came across as threatening.”

**Explain**

A person may have learned to protect themselves through strong language, posture, volume, or distance.

That can explain the behavior.

It does not erase its impact.

**Separate**

Separate:

* what I intended
* what I actually did
* how the other person experienced it
* what consequence followed

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What can I change without giving up what I was trying to accomplish?”**

**Practice**

Instead of raising your voice:

> “I need some space. Please give me a second.”

The need remains.

The delivery changes.

**Carry It**

> **“My intention explains my behavior. It does not erase its impact.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My words, tone, posture, behavior, and willingness to adjust when I see that my approach created unnecessary conflict.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot control another person's interpretation or guarantee that everyone will understand my intention.

### 7. Next Action

When I need something from another person, state the need directly before allowing frustration to communicate it for me.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Mean what you mean. Watch what your behavior communicates.**

## Reentry Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Intention and impact are not the same thing.**

Good intentions do not automatically produce good outcomes.

Recognizing the difference gives you a chance to repair the outcome and change the behavior.

### 2. Environmental Translation

During reentry, a person may genuinely intend to protect themselves, maintain independence, or avoid conflict.

But family members, employers, landlords, or community members may experience the behavior differently.

If the participant only argues about intention, the actual problem remains.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A person stops answering calls because they do not want to argue with family.

The family interprets the silence as disrespect or abandonment.

The person's intention was to avoid conflict.

The impact is that communication breaks down.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when a behavior intended to prevent a problem creates another problem.

**Name**

> “I was trying to avoid conflict, but my silence made the situation worse.”

**Explain**

Avoiding confrontation may once have been a useful way to stay safe or prevent escalation.

That explains the choice.

It does not make the result disappear.

**Separate**

Separate:

* intention from outcome
* taking space from disappearing
* avoiding conflict from avoiding responsibility
* explanation from excuse

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“How can I accomplish the same goal without creating a new problem?”**

**Practice**

Instead of disappearing:

> “I don't want to argue right now. I'll call you tomorrow.”

Then follow through.

**Carry It**

> **“I can protect my peace without making other people guess what happened.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

Communicating clearly, following through, and repairing the damage caused by my behavior when appropriate.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I am not responsible for another person's feelings, assumptions, or response.

### 7. Next Action

When I need space, communicate that directly instead of leaving people to interpret my silence.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Good intentions still require responsible behavior.**

## Veteran Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Intention and impact are not the same thing.**

A behavior can come from a useful intention and still create an unwanted result.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Directness, efficiency, correction, or rapid problem-solving may have been useful in previous environments.

In civilian settings, the same behavior may be interpreted as hostility, impatience, or disrespect.

The goal is not to abandon the strength.

It is to understand its impact and translate it.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

A coworker makes a mistake.

The participant immediately gives blunt instructions because they want to solve the problem quickly.

The coworker feels embarrassed and becomes defensive.

The participant thinks:

> “I was just trying to help.”

That may be true.

The impact still matters.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when a useful intention produces unnecessary friction.

**Name**

> “I was trying to solve the problem, but my delivery made the person defensive.”

**Explain**

Direct communication can be efficient in some environments.

Different environments may require a different delivery.

**Separate**

Separate:

* efficiency from urgency
* directness from hostility
* correction from humiliation
* intention from impact

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“Can I keep the useful part of this response while changing the way I deliver it?”**

**Practice**

Try:

> “I noticed a problem. Want to work through it together?”

Instead of immediately taking control.

**Carry It**

> **“Keep the purpose. Adjust the delivery.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

How I communicate and whether I adjust when my approach is creating unnecessary conflict.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot control whether someone responds positively to reasonable feedback.

### 7. Next Action

Before correcting someone, consider whether the same message can be delivered more effectively.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **A good purpose does not require a poor delivery.**

## Workplace Track

### 1. Core Connection

**Intention and impact are not the same thing.**

You can mean well and still create a problem.

You can also recognize the problem without turning yourself into a bad person.

### 2. Environmental Translation

Workplaces constantly translate behavior.

An employee may intend to be efficient but appear impatient.

They may intend to be honest but sound dismissive.

They may intend to give someone space but appear disengaged.

The participant needs to learn to account for both intention and impact.

### 3. Recognizable Scenario

An employee gives a short answer during a meeting because they are trying to stay focused.

A coworker interprets the response as rude.

The employee says:

> “I wasn't being rude.”

Again, intention and impact are different questions.

### 4. Seven-Step Application

**Recognize**

Notice when another person's response tells you that your behavior landed differently than you intended.

**Name**

> “I did not intend to be dismissive, but I can see that my response sounded that way.”

**Explain**

Pressure, concentration, frustration, or learned communication habits can affect delivery.

They may explain the behavior.

They do not make impact irrelevant.

**Separate**

Separate:

* intention from impact
* explanation from excuse
* feedback from personal attack
* one communication mistake from personal identity

**Interrupt**

Ask:

> **“What did the other person actually hear?”**

**Practice**

Instead of defending the intention immediately:

> “I didn't mean it that way. Let me say that differently.”

Then say it differently.

**Carry It**

> **“I do not have to choose between defending my intention and correcting my impact.”**

### 5. Accountability Check

> **What part belongs to me?**

My communication and my willingness to correct it.

### 6. Boundary Check

> **What part does not belong to me?**

I cannot control another person's feelings or guarantee that every interaction will be understood perfectly.

### 7. Next Action

When someone says my behavior had an unintended impact, listen for the information before defending my intention.

### 8. Carry-Forward

> **Listen to the impact without lying about the intention.**
