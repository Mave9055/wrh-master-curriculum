# WHERE CAN THE LOOP BE INTERRUPTED?

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

looked back at a situation and thought:

> *"I knew this was going bad."*

You noticed the first sign.

You felt yourself getting angry.

You saw the message you didn't want to answer.

You started thinking about leaving.

You knew you were getting overwhelmed.

But you kept going.

Then something happened.

Now the problem is much bigger.

And afterward you think:

> *"I should have stopped this earlier."*

Maybe you should have.

But here's the useful question:

**Where could you have changed direction?**

---

### 2. NAME

That point is an:

## **Interruption point.**

An interruption point is simply **a place in a pattern where you can do something different.**

Every loop has more than one possible stopping point.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Think about a row of dominoes.

One falls.

Then another.

Then another.

If you wait until the last domino, stopping the chain is hard.

But if you catch the chain early, one small move can change what happens next.

People work in patterns too.

Something happens.

You react.

Then another thing happens.

You react again.

Soon you're dealing with the result of ten small steps.

You don't have to wait until step ten.

Look for step two.

Or step three.

**Earlier is usually easier.**

---

### 4. SEPARATE

You won't catch every loop early.

Sometimes you won't notice until the damage is already done.

That's not a reason to give up.

You can still interrupt the next step.

Accountability isn't about being perfect.

It's about becoming more aware of where your choices begin.

The goal is not:

> **"I will never lose control again."**

The goal is:

> **"I'll learn to catch myself sooner."**

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

Ask:

> **"What's the next step?"**

Not:

*"How do I fix everything?"*

Just:

> **What's the next step?**

If you're angry, the next step might be lowering your voice.

If you're avoiding a call, the next step might be opening the phone.

If you're overwhelmed, the next step might be sitting down.

If you've made a mistake, the next step might be admitting it.

One step changes the next step.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Run this sequence in your head:

**Someone says something.**

↓

**You get angry.**

↓

**You raise your voice.**

↓

**They raise theirs.**

↓

**Now you're both fighting.**

Where could you interrupt it?

Maybe before you answer.

Maybe after the first sentence.

Maybe when your voice starts rising.

Maybe when you notice their voice rising.

Pick **one point.**

Now choose one different action.

For example:

> **"I'm getting angry. Give me a minute."**

That's the practice.

Not controlling the whole situation.

Finding one place where you still have a choice.

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **You don't have to stop the whole loop; find the next place where you can change direction.**
