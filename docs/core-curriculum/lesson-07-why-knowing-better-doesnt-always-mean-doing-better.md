# WHY KNOWING BETTER DOESN'T ALWAYS MEAN DOING BETTER

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

known exactly what you needed to do.

You knew.

Nobody had to explain it.

You even explained it to yourself.

*"I need to make that call."*

*"I need to get some sleep."*

*"I need to stop arguing."*

*"I need to take care of this before it gets worse."*

And then you don't do it.

You sit there.

You think about doing it.

You may even feel worse because you're not doing it.

But getting started feels like trying to push a truck uphill.

Then somebody tells you:

> **"You know what you're supposed to do."**

And you think:

> *"Yeah. I fucking know."*

That's the part people often miss.

**Knowing isn't always the same thing as being able to act.**

---

### 2. NAME

We can call this:

## **The knowing-doing gap.**

You know what would probably help.

But knowing it doesn't automatically make the action easy.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Doing something takes more than information.

You also have to start.

Focus.

Choose.

Move.

Deal with whatever happens next.

When you're overloaded, tired, angry, worried, or stuck in your head, even a simple task can feel much bigger than it looks from the outside.

That can create a strange situation:

**You understand the answer.**

**You still can't seem to start.**

That doesn't mean the task doesn't matter.

It means the distance between **knowing** and **doing** needs attention.

---

### 4. SEPARATE

Understanding the gap doesn't remove responsibility.

If something needs to get done, it still needs to get done.

But calling yourself lazy, stupid, or worthless usually doesn't make starting easier.

The better question is:

> **"What's making the first step so hard?"**

Then make the first step smaller.

You don't need to win the entire battle before you begin.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

## **Make the first step almost too small to fail.**

Don't:

> "Clean the house."

Try:

> **"Pick up one thing."**

Don't:

> "Fix my finances."

Try:

> **"Open the bill."**

Don't:

> "Get my life together."

Try:

> **"Set one alarm."**

The goal is movement.

Once you're moving, the next step becomes easier to see.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Imagine you have an important form to complete.

You keep putting it off.

The form feels huge.

Instead of:

> *"I need to finish this."*

Break it down.

**Step 1:** Find the form.

**Step 2:** Open it.

**Step 3:** Read the first question.

That's all.

You don't have to finish it in the exercise.

You're practicing something different:

**Turning a giant task into a small action.**

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **Knowing what to do is the map; taking one small step is how you start moving.**
