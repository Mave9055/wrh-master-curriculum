# WHEN YOUR BODY REACTS BEFORE YOU CAN THINK

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

heard a door slam and suddenly felt your whole body tighten?

Someone raises their voice.

You don't know what they're going to say next.

Your heart starts beating harder.

Your jaw tightens.

You are suddenly paying attention to every movement in the room.

Then somebody says:

> "Hey. You okay?"

And you realize you were already reacting before you had even decided what you thought was happening.

Or maybe someone asks you a simple question.

You know the answer.

You've answered it a hundred times.

But suddenly your mind goes blank.

You stand there trying to find the words.

Nothing comes.

Then later, when you're alone, the answer comes to you immediately.

You think:

*"Why the hell couldn't I say that five minutes ago?"*

Because sometimes **your body gets there before your thinking does.**

---

### 2. NAME

We can call this:

## **The body-first reaction.**

Something happens.

Your body reacts.

Then your thinking catches up.

The reaction can happen so quickly that it feels like you had no choice at all.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Your body is constantly taking in information.

Sounds.

Faces.

Movement.

Tone of voice.

Distance.

Your surroundings.

It uses that information to decide whether you need to pay attention.

That process can happen very quickly.

Sometimes faster than conscious thought.

That's useful when you need to move quickly.

But it can also create problems.

Your body may react to a situation before you have enough information to understand it.

That's why you might suddenly feel tense before you even know why.

Or go quiet before you know what you want to say.

Or want to leave before you've decided whether leaving is actually the best choice.

**The reaction comes first. The explanation may come later.**

---

### 4. SEPARATE

Again:

**A reaction is not a command.**

Feeling angry does not require you to attack.

Feeling afraid does not require you to run.

Feeling overwhelmed does not require you to disappear.

Going blank does not mean you have to stay silent forever.

You may not control the first reaction.

You can work on what happens **after** it.

That's where choice comes back into the picture.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

Use one simple move:

## **Give yourself five seconds.**

Don't make the big decision yet.

Take one breath.

Look at one object in the room.

Then ask:

> **"What do I actually know?"**

Not what you think is going to happen.

Not what happened last time.

What do you actually know **right now**?

Five seconds can create room for your thinking to catch up.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Imagine you're sitting across from someone.

They say:

> **"We need to talk about what happened yesterday."**

Your body reacts immediately.

Maybe your chest tightens.

Maybe your first thought is:

*"Here we go."*

Before you answer, take five seconds.

Look at something in the room.

Ask:

**What do I actually know?**

You know:

> They want to talk about yesterday.

That's all.

You don't know yet:

* what they're going to say
* what they think
* what they want
* what happens next

Practice staying with what you **know** instead of filling in everything you don't.

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **A fast reaction is information—not an order.**
