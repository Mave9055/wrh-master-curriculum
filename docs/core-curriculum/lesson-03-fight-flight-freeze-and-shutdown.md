# FIGHT, FLIGHT, FREEZE & SHUTDOWN

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

gotten angry so fast that your mouth was moving before your brain caught up?

Maybe somebody says something that hits the wrong nerve.

Your voice gets louder.

You step closer.

You want to win the argument.

You don't want to back down.

That's one response.

But maybe yours goes the other direction.

The argument starts.

You leave.

You stop answering texts.

You avoid the person.

You tell yourself you'll deal with it tomorrow.

Tomorrow comes.

You still don't deal with it.

That's another response.

Or maybe you stay.

You hear the words.

You know you're supposed to answer.

But nothing comes out.

You can't think of what to say.

You feel stuck.

And sometimes it goes even further.

You are physically there, but it feels like you're not really participating in what's happening.

Four different reactions.

One important fact:

**People don't all react to pressure the same way.**

---

### 2. NAME

Four common patterns are:

## **Fight**

Move toward the problem.

## **Flight**

Move away from the problem.

## **Freeze**

Get stuck and have trouble deciding what to do.

## **Shutdown**

Pull inward or go mentally quiet when the situation becomes too much.

These are plain-language names for things people experience every day.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

When your system decides something is too much, it looks for a way to handle it.

One person gets louder.

Another walks away.

Another can't make a decision.

Another becomes very quiet and disconnected from what's happening.

None of these reactions automatically tells us **why** the person is doing it.

And the same person can react differently at different times.

The important thing is recognizing the pattern.

Because if you don't recognize it, the reaction can take over the rest of the situation.

---

### 4. SEPARATE

Knowing your reaction has a name does not make every reaction acceptable.

Anger doesn't give you permission to hurt someone.

Leaving doesn't mean you never have to return to the problem.

Freezing doesn't mean the problem disappears.

Shutting down doesn't remove the consequences of what happens next.

**Understanding gives you a map.**

It doesn't drive the vehicle for you.

You still have a part to play.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

When you notice yourself moving into one of these patterns:

## **Name the direction.**

Ask yourself:

> **"Am I moving toward this, away from this, stuck in it, or going quiet?"**

You don't have to fix everything.

Just identify the direction.

Once you can see the pattern, look for **one small action in the opposite direction.**

Angry?

Lower your voice.

Running?

Stay long enough to send one message.

Frozen?

Choose one next step.

Shutting down?

Look around and name where you are.

Small is enough.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Imagine you have an appointment tomorrow.

You haven't prepared.

You start thinking about everything that could go wrong.

You feel yourself wanting to cancel.

Run the four possibilities:

**Fight:**
*"Forget them. I'm not going."*

**Flight:**
*"I'll just avoid the appointment."*

**Freeze:**
*"I don't know what to do, so I'll do nothing."*

**Shutdown:**
*"I don't want to think about any of this."*

Now try something different.

Ask:

> **"What's one move that takes me toward handling this?"**

Maybe it's putting the appointment time on your phone.

Maybe it's finding your clothes.

Maybe it's setting one alarm.

You don't have to solve tomorrow.

**You only have to make one move toward it.**

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **You don't have to control the first reaction; you can choose the next move.**
