# SHAME, AVOIDANCE & THE REINFORCEMENT LOOP

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

missed something important.

You know you should deal with it.

But now you're embarrassed.

You don't want to call.

You don't want to explain.

You don't want to hear what they're going to say.

So you wait.

One day passes.

Then another.

Now you're even more embarrassed.

The phone rings.

You don't answer.

You see the message.

You don't open it.

The problem gets bigger.

And the bigger it gets, the harder it feels to face.

Eventually you're not avoiding the original problem anymore.

You're avoiding the **feeling you expect when you face it.**

That's how a small problem can become a wall.

---

### 2. NAME

There are two simple names for this:

## **Shame**

and

## **Avoidance.**

Shame says:

> **"Something is wrong with me."**

Avoidance says:

> **"If I don't deal with it, I don't have to feel this right now."**

Together, they can create a loop.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Avoiding something can bring short-term relief.

You don't make the call.

For a moment, you feel better.

You don't open the message.

The pressure drops.

You don't walk into the office.

You don't have to face the conversation.

Your brain notices that relief.

So the next time the same kind of situation happens, avoidance can feel like the fastest way out.

But the problem is still there.

Now there may be another problem too:

**You feel worse about yourself for avoiding it.**

That can lead to more avoidance.

And the loop gets stronger.

---

### 4. SEPARATE

Making a mistake does not make you a bad person.

Avoiding a problem does not make it disappear.

And understanding why you avoided something does not mean you don't have to deal with the result.

**Responsibility starts where avoidance ends.**

You can say:

> *"I screwed this up."*

without saying:

> *"I'm a screwup."*

Those are not the same thing.

One describes something you did.

The other turns it into who you are.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

## **Do the thing before you feel ready.**

Not the whole thing.

Just one honest move.

If you missed the appointment:

> **Call and ask what you need to do next.**

If you ignored the message:

> **Open it.**

If you owe someone an answer:

> **Send the first sentence.**

You don't need the perfect explanation.

You need an opening.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Imagine you missed an appointment.

You didn't call.

Now it's three days later.

You see the number on your phone.

Your first thought:

> *"They're going to be pissed."*

You want to ignore it again.

Instead, practice the smallest honest move.

You call.

If they answer, you don't need a speech.

You can say:

> **"I missed the appointment. What do I need to do now?"**

That's it.

No excuses.

No dramatic confession.

No beating yourself up.

Just:

**What happened.**

**What needs to happen next.**

That's how you step out of the loop.

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **You don't have to feel better before you act; sometimes acting is how you start feeling better.**
