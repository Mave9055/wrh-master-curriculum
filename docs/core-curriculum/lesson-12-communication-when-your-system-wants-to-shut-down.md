# COMMUNICATION WHEN YOUR SYSTEM WANTS TO SHUT DOWN

### 1. RECOGNIZE

**Have you ever...**

been asked a simple question and suddenly couldn't find the words?

Someone is waiting for an answer.

You know you need to respond.

You may even know exactly what you want to say.

But your mind feels empty.

The other person keeps waiting.

Now you're more uncomfortable.

They ask again:

> "Are you going to answer me?"

You want to say:

> *"I am trying."*

But nothing comes out.

Or maybe you do the opposite.

You send a long message because talking feels impossible.

You type.

Delete.

Type again.

Delete again.

Finally you send nothing.

Then the silence creates another problem.

---

### 2. NAME

This is:

## **Communication shutdown.**

It means you have trouble getting your words out when the situation becomes too much.

It can look like silence.

Avoiding a conversation.

Going blank.

Walking away.

Or not knowing how to explain what you need.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Communication takes work.

You have to listen.

Understand.

Choose words.

Decide what matters.

Then say it.

When you're overloaded, that process can become harder.

The more pressure you feel, the harder it may become to find the right words.

Then the other person sees silence.

They may think:

> *"He's refusing to answer."*

You may be thinking:

> *"I can't get the words out."*

Now the misunderstanding creates even more pressure.

And more pressure can make communication even harder.

---

### 4. SEPARATE

You don't have to give a perfect answer.

You don't have to explain your entire life.

But silence can create consequences too.

So the goal is not:

> **"Say everything."**

The goal is:

> **"Give the other person enough information to know what happens next."**

That can be one sentence.

You can still be accountable while taking a moment to collect yourself.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

Keep one sentence available:

> **"I need a minute, but I'm not ignoring you."**

That's it.

It tells the other person two important things:

**You heard them.**

**You're not ready to answer yet.**

If you can communicate those two things, you've kept the conversation alive.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Imagine someone asks:

> **"Why didn't you do what you said you would do?"**

Your mind goes blank.

You don't have to invent an explanation.

Practice the one sentence:

> **"I need a minute to answer that honestly."**

Now imagine they say:

> "Fine."

You take the minute.

Then answer what you can.

Maybe:

> **"I didn't do it. I put it off."**

That's enough to begin.

You don't need a perfect speech.

You need an honest opening.

---

### 7. CARRY IT

> **When the words won't come, communicate one thing: "I'm here, and I'll answer."**
