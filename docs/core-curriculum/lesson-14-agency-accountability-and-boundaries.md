# Agency, Accountability & Boundaries

## Before You Begin

Responsibility can become distorted in two opposite directions.

One person blames everyone else for everything.

Another person blames themselves for everything.

Neither position creates real accountability.

Accountability means being honest about **what belongs to you**.

Agency means recognizing that your choices still matter.

Boundaries mean recognizing where your responsibility ends.

These three ideas belong together.

---

## Why This Matters

Survival can teach a person to react.

Later, that same person may have difficulty distinguishing between:

> **“This happened to me.”**

and

> **“This is now mine to deal with.”**

Both can be true.

Something can happen to you that was not your fault.

You can still have responsibility for what you do afterward.

That is not blame.

That is agency.

---

### 1. RECOGNIZE

Notice the moments when responsibility becomes distorted.

You may notice:

* blaming someone else for every decision you make
* taking responsibility for things other people chose
* apologizing for things that were not yours
* refusing to apologize when something actually was yours
* believing someone else's behavior gives you permission to behave badly
* feeling responsible for another person's emotions
* believing boundaries are selfish
* believing accountability means accepting punishment or shame

These are different problems.

They require different responses.

---

### 2. NAME

Use clear language.

> **“That happened to me.”**

> **“I did that.”**

> **“That was their choice.”**

> **“That part is mine.”**

> **“That part is not mine.”**

These sentences may sound simple.

They are powerful because they separate facts from blame.

---

### 3. EXPLAIN

Agency does not mean complete control.

You cannot control what someone else thinks.

You cannot control whether someone forgives you.

You cannot control whether someone treats you fairly.

You cannot control the past.

But you can influence what you do next.

You can choose whether to tell the truth.

You can choose whether to keep escalating.

You can choose whether to make restitution when appropriate.

You can choose whether to leave a situation.

You can choose whether to ask for help.

You can choose whether to repeat a behavior.

That is agency.

Accountability is not:

> “Everything is my fault.”

Accountability is:

> **“I will own what is mine.”**

---

### 4. SEPARATE

Separate four things:

**What happened to me.**

**What I chose.**

**What someone else chose.**

**What I choose now.**

The past matters.

But the past does not get automatic ownership of the present.

If someone treated you badly, that does not make your harmful response harmless.

If you made a mistake, that does not make you a worthless person.

If someone hurt you, you do not have to protect them from the consequences of their choices.

If you hurt someone, you do not get to hide behind what happened to you.

Context matters.

So does responsibility.

---

### 5. INTERRUPT

When you hear yourself saying:

> “They made me do it.”

Stop.

Replace it with:

> **“They influenced the situation. I still have to own my response.”**

When you hear:

> “Everything is my fault.”

Stop.

Ask:

> **“What part is actually mine?”**

When you hear:

> “I have to fix this for them.”

Stop.

Ask:

> **“Did I cause this, or am I trying to control their outcome?”**

These interruptions restore proportion.

---

### 6. PRACTICE

Choose one current conflict.

Divide it into four columns:

| Question                          | Answer |
| --------------------------------- | ------ |
| What happened to me?              |        |
| What did I do?                    |        |
| What did the other person choose? |        |
| What can I choose now?            |        |

Then identify one boundary.

Complete this sentence:

> **“I am responsible for __________, but I am not responsible for __________.”**

Examples:

> “I am responsible for what I say. I am not responsible for making someone agree with me.”

> “I am responsible for my behavior. I am not responsible for controlling another adult's behavior.”

> “I am responsible for repairing what I damaged. I am not responsible for pretending nothing happened.”

A boundary is not a threat.

It is a clear statement about what you will and will not participate in.

---

### 7. CARRY IT

You do not need to carry everybody.

You do not need to escape responsibility either.

Carry what belongs to you.

Put down what does not.

Own your choices without turning yourself into your worst mistake.

Hold other people accountable without making yourself responsible for their lives.

**Agency is not control.**

**Accountability is not shame.**

**Boundaries are not cruelty.**

They are ways of keeping responsibility in the right place.

---

## Common Misunderstandings

**“If I understand why I did something, am I excusing it?”**

No.

Understanding explains.

Accountability evaluates what you did and what you do next.

You can understand yourself and still say:

> “I was wrong.”

**“Does accountability mean I have to accept whatever someone does to me?”**

No.

Taking responsibility for your actions does not require surrendering your boundaries.

**“Does setting a boundary mean someone has to agree with it?”**

No.

A boundary is about what **you** will do.

---

## Permission

You are allowed to say:

> “That was not mine.”

You are allowed to say:

> “That part was mine.”

You are allowed to apologize without humiliating yourself.

You are allowed to refuse responsibility that belongs to someone else.

You are allowed to leave situations that repeatedly violate your boundaries.

You are allowed to be accountable without being destroyed by shame.

---

## Reflection Questions

1. What am I currently taking responsibility for that is not actually mine?
2. What am I avoiding responsibility for that is mine?
3. What choice do I still have?
4. Where do I need a boundary?
5. What would accountability look like without self-punishment?

---

## Key Takeaways

* Being affected by something does not remove agency.
* Understanding behavior does not excuse behavior.
* Accountability means owning your part.
* Boundaries define where your responsibility ends.
* Other people's choices belong to them.
* Your next choice belongs to you.

---

## Carry This With You

> **Own what is yours.
> Return what is not.
> Choose what comes next.**
